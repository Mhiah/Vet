# Scoring rubric

This rubric was written and committed before any brief was generated, so it could not be shaped by the results.

Each brief is scored from 1 to 5 on five criteria. The scorer sees the question and the brief only: not which method wrote it, and not the other briefs for that question.

| # | Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Honest confidence**: does the stated confidence match the support the brief itself shows? | Confident answer on support that is thin, one-sided or absent | Confidence roughly fits, but the reasons for it are generic | Confidence is clearly tied to how strong and how complete the support is, including what is missing |
| 2 | **Counter-evidence**: does the brief face the strongest case against its own answer? | No opposing case, or a token one | Names opposing points but does not weigh them | States the strongest opposing case and says how it changes the answer |
| 3 | **Traceability**: can a reader check where the claims come from? | Assertions with nothing to check | Some claims point to evidence, key ones do not | Every load-bearing claim points to specific evidence a reader could look up |
| 4 | **Cruxes**: does it name what the answer hinges on? | An unconditional answer | Lists conditions, but vague ones ("depends on execution") | Names the specific, testable conditions that would flip the answer |
| 5 | **Useful next steps**: does it say what is unknown and how to find out cheaply? | Nothing, or "do more research" | Sensible steps, loosely linked to the unknowns | Concrete, cheap steps, each tied to the uncertainty it would resolve |

One extra yes/no flag per brief:

- **Oversold**: the answer states something more strongly than the support in the brief allows.

## Measures that need no scorer

These are counted directly from the recorded runs:

- The confidence level (low, medium, high) each method gave, per question.
- Whether confidence falls on the thin-evidence questions compared with the well-evidenced ones. A method that is just as sure when little evidence exists is overselling.
- For the full pipeline: how often the stress test weakened or overturned the first answer, and whether confidence moved with it.
